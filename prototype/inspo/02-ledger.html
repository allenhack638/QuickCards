Ledger

QuickCards as a private-bank statement: warm paper, ink, fine rules and one red mark. Each card is a folio, a coloured masthead over a ruled sheet where the number, expiry, CVV and name sit in their own cells, each one tap from the clipboard. Type does the work that chrome usually does. The serif names things, the sans runs the interface, and every digit is set in mono so a number reads the same at the till as it does on the plastic. Light is the home key, because a statement is read in daylight. Dark is the same document after hours: charcoal paper, ink reversed, same rules.

Tap count, app open to copied number: open the app → fingerprint (system prompt, no tap) → tap the card number cell. One tap. Expiry, CVV and name are also one tap each, because every folio on Home is open. Inside the instant-copy window there is no prompt at all.

Live: tap a value to copy it; hold the number for 450 ms to reveal it.

  1. Set it like a statement.Hierarchy by type and rules, not boxes. A serif title, a 1 px ink rule, then the figures. No cards inside cards, no grey slabs.
  2. The value is the target.Copy targets are whole ruled cells, 64 dp and up. The last four are the heaviest figures on the card; masked groups step back to the third ink.
  3. One red mark per page.Red is the primary button, or the Copied stamp on Home where there is no primary button. Selection, focus and switches are ink.

What changes versus the current QuickCards

  • The pass becomes a folio. Band colour moves into a full masthead with a serif bank name and a monogram; values sit in a ruled spec sheet instead of a perforated stub.
  • Three typefaces with jobs. Newsreader for names and titles, Inter Tight for the interface, IBM Plex Mono for every digit. Five roles with real steps replace eleven sizes.
  • Ink selection, real edges. Selected chips are ink-filled with a check; every interactive outline is ≥ 3:1; switches and checkboxes are ink, never red.
  • Labels above, always. Fields keep M3 OutlinedTextField with the label above the box, helper lines reserved, errors with an icon and the fix.
  • Decisions look like decisions. Dialogs about a card show the card; destructive confirms are outlined in the error colour; consequential ones outlined in ink; the delete copy no longer contradicts its Undo.

Colour

Warm paper with chroma held near zero (about 0.006 in OKLCH) so it never reads yellow on an LCD; ink is a warm near-black. The ladder has six steps and the card object sits differently per theme: white paper on the light ground, one tonal step up on the dark ground. Brand red is a fill only, under white text (5.68:1). It never appears as small text on dark.

Surface ladder

Text, rules, states, error, brand

Card band palette, retuned

All sixteen bands are normalised so white text is at least 8.29:1, and the secondary line on the masthead is white mixed 78 % into the band (≥ 5.78:1), tinted from the band rather than grey. Teal, Olive, Moss, Burnt orange, Crimson, Magenta, Violet, Forest and Rust were darkened 4 to 10 % in lightness; the rest are unchanged. Slate is kept for stored cards only.

Network plates

Simple typographic stand-ins on a white plate (no trademark artwork). Custom networks are a text label in a 1 px white frame.

Typography

Newsreader (optical-size axis) names things: screen titles, bank names, dialog titles, section heads, empty states. It never sets a digit: when a title holds a figure (“Update 2 cards?”) the figure is a span in Inter Tight, and a query is a span in Plex Mono. Inter Tight runs the interface. IBM Plex Mono sets every value a person reads aloud or copies, plus the small value labels on the folio, where it is a measurement label and not a costume. All three ship in res/font; tabular figures (tnum) are on everywhere.

Shape, spacing and elevation

Small radii are the editorial tell: 2 for stamps and status, 4 for fields and snackbars, 6 for folios and buttons, 8 for dialogs, 10 for the closed card face, 12 for sheet tops, full only for chips and avatars. Spacing is an 8 dp grid with a 4 dp half-step, 20 dp screen gutters and more space above a section head than below it.

Motion

Chrome moves on M3 standard timing; one moment is authored, the copy stamp. Everything starts from a visible default and eases out exponentially. With Remove animations on, every movement becomes an instant change or a short fade, and the reveal timer shows seconds instead of a draining rule.

Iconography

Material Symbols Rounded, one setting: weight 400, grade 0 on light and −25 on dark, optical size 24, outline at rest and filled only for the selected nav item and the pin. Icons sit in the second ink; nothing is tinted except the error icon.

Card rendering recipe

There are two drawings of one card. The folio (open) is what Home shows: masthead plus ruled spec sheet, every value a copy target. The face (closed, ISO ID-1) is the card as an object, used where the card is context rather than a tool: the form preview, the ⋮ sheet header, dialogs, autofill rows. Both are built from the same five layers.

Six bands, expired, archived and placeholder

Buttons

Four levels and two warnings. Filled red is the one primary per screen; filled ink is never used for actions (it is reserved for selection). Pressed buttons tighten their corner from 6 to 3 dp, the M3 Expressive shape morph in this direction's vocabulary. Every button has a 48 dp target even when its container is 44.

Chips and status

Holder chips carry the holder's initials and become ink-filled with a check when selected, so “All” finally has a non-colour cue too. Input chips have their own remove target. Status is set as a stamp (Expired, outlined) or a plain label with an icon (Expires soon, Check details, Archived).

Text fields

Label above, box below, helper line reserved underneath. This is M3 OutlinedTextField with labelPosition = TextFieldLabelPosition.Above() (Material3 1.4); the box keeps the 56 dp height, 4 dp corner and outline roles. Focus is a 2 dp ink stroke and a whisper of warm tint; error is a 2 dp error stroke, an icon and words that name the fix.

Selection controls

Ink, not red. Checkbox, radio and switch states are carried by shape (check, dot, thumb size, check icon on the thumb) as well as fill.

The folio, every state

Interactive: tap any value to copy it, hold the number or CVV for 450 ms to reveal it for 5 s. Keyboard: Tab to a cell, Enter copies, Alt+Enter reveals.

Usability & accessibility

The bar is that a person using TalkBack, a person at 200 % text and a person with one thumb at a till all finish the job as fast as anyone else. Everything below is measured from this page, not asserted.

Contrast, computed

Ratios are computed live from the tokens in this file (WCAG 2.2 relative luminance). Targets: card values and primary labels AAA 7:1; secondary text AA 4.5:1; outlines, rings and tracks 3:1. Rules inside the folio are decorative and exempt; every interactive edge uses the outline role.

Text at 200 %

Sizes follow Android 14's non-linear font-scale table for 2.0 (14 sp becomes 26, 34 sp titles become 39), so headings stay on screen while body text doubles. The folio reflows: the masthead wraps, the number wraps between digit groups and never inside one, the stub becomes two columns and then one, and nothing that identifies a card is ever truncated. Toggle “Text at 200 %” in the sidebar to apply it to every frame on the page.

Touch targets and the thumb zone

Boxes are measured from the rendered layout in this page (green ≥ 48 dp, red below). The band marks the comfortable one-handed zone: every copy target on the first folio and the primary actions of each screen land in or near it.

TalkBack: one stop per card

Each folio is one focus stop with a spoken summary and custom actions, so ten cards are ten swipes, not a hundred. Individual cells stay reachable by Explore by touch with the exact strings from strings_cards.xml. Sheets and dialogs set a pane title and move focus to it; closing returns focus to the trigger.
